## Position

1. **Current position:** R11 is my conditional recommendation. I have no defensible runner-up.

2. **Strongest support:** R11 is the only card where a later cold role changes an action because of what another role's memory learned, and where the evidence path is relational:
   - Agent B starts cold. It follows claim → proposed support → the exact source span.
   - It finds that "ready" traces to one conditional observation ("new tenants, after migration").
   - It asks a question specific to that condition.
   - The reviewed qualifier is stored with its scope, so a later cold Sales session answers customer X correctly.
   - The card keeps READ, proposed support and reviewed support separate, and root counts never authorize anything.

3. **Strongest counter:**
   - With at most 8 items per case, a control given source and origin IDs plus the same LLM sees everything, so the graph's selection role may be invisible at fixture scale.
   - V6, Graphiti and Glean also leave R11 no claim to novel mechanism.
   - The three layers of support are hard to explain in 30 seconds.

4. **Dependent assumption:** Role-agent restatements drop qualifiers often enough to matter, and one reviewer action is reused enough to justify recording lineage.

5. **What would change my mind:** I would reject R11 if a configured control matches its clarification and its change to the later session with less preparation. The control would be Graphiti or Glean-style provenance, or source plus origin IDs, with the same LLM. I would also reject it if the paper oracle shows that background READ links collapse separate observations, or that a reviewed qualifier leaks to an unrelated scope.

6. **Direct reply to the strongest opposing argument:** The argument is that V6 plus capable controls ask equally good questions. I accept this as the decisive gate. Matching on correctness alone is fine. What would justify rejection is equal usefulness with less preparation.
   - R11's claimed edge is narrow: at runtime, the graph picks which span and scope reach agent B, and the reviewed scoped qualifier stays in memory across roles.
   - If that only shows up at a scale beyond the fixture, it is an empirical risk the owner must accept, not a design flaw.

## Fatal flaws vs empirical concerns

**R11** has no fatal design flaw. Three empirical concerns need owner acceptance:
- **Usefulness:** whether a control matches it with less preparation.
- **Presentation:** a plain-language headline, with the support layers kept in the details view.
- **Review cost:** whether recording lineage and reviewing support costs more than the reuse saves.

**R03 and R06** have differentiation flaws that are effectively fatal under the "equal usefulness with less preparation" gate:
- **R03:** the only part left after V13 is a single indirect dependent path.
- **R06:** the remaining hook is a two-hop acceptance check that a signed checklist plus Gong/Gainsight already does.

## Exact mind-change since the attack stage

I withdraw R03 as runner-up. V13's Flamekeeper, Implix and Culturro pages advertise the exact workflow: discover gaps, run targeted interviews, hand reviewed knowledge to a successor. Their pages don't prove they work equally well. But the "precise observed dependency" hook that's left is too thin to differentiate.

I'm not reopening another card. Rejecting everything stays on the table if R11's control gate fails.
